How this works
  • Projected points use this league's scoring. They come from each player's role (snap, target, carry, red-zone and tackle share), recent scoring, how steady he is week to week, Vegas lines and the matchup. The model trains on every NFL game since 2019.
  • The bar shows a player's likely range. The thin line runs from a bad week (10th percentile) to a big week (90th), and the thick part is a normal week (25th to 75th). The dot is the number used to rank him. The dashed line is replacement level (a fringe starter), so green means better than that. For rest of season and playoffs the bar shows a typical week.
  • Rank for moves the dot. Safe floor ranks players by a bad-but-not-awful week, Upside by a big week, and Balanced by the average. Use Safe when you are favored and Upside when you need a big week.
  • Value is points above a fringe starter: fill 12 teams' starting lineups, then take the next three players at the position. IDP uses one level for DL, LB and DB because the D slot takes any of them. The Waivers tab compares free agents with your own weakest starter instead, per game for rest of season.
  • Projected points never change with Rank for; only the order and the dot move. Tags. SAFE means even a bad week (25th percentile) is at replacement level. BOOM means at least a 1 in 4 chance of a big week. K and DB projections are the least reliable in testing. Big-week lines: QB 25, RB 20, WR 20, TE 15, K 13, DL 10, LB 12, DB 11 points.
  • Chance to play combines the injury report, Yahoo tags, IR status and missed games. It is already built into the numbers.
