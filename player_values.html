How the model works
  • Data. Every NFL player-game from 2018 to now (nflverse), scored with this league's settings. 2018 only feeds priors; training starts in 2019.
  • Usage first. Snap share, target share, carry share, red-zone shares, air-yards share and pass-attempt share on offense; snap share, tackles per opponent play, and sack, QB-hit, TFL and pass-defended rates for IDP. Each is a recency-weighted rate (8-week and 3-week half-lives; last season counts about a third) pulled toward the position average by empirical Bayes. The pull is sized from how much players truly differ, so a two-game spike moves a player less than a ten-game trend.
  • Consistency. Over each player's last 16 games: spread of weekly points, 20th and 80th percentile games, and shrunk rates of start-worthy, boom and bust weeks.
  • Context. Vegas implied team total, spread, home, rest, dome and what the opponent allows to the position. Weeks without odds use team scoring strength.
  • Models. Gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM), one set for offense and one for IDP: a mean projection, five quantiles (10/25/50/75/90) and two classifiers for P(start-worthy week) and P(boom week), calibrated with isotonic regression on a held-out season. Injury and roster status mix in a chance of zero.
  • Modes. Consistency blends the projection toward the 25th percentile; Ceiling blends it toward the 90th. Value is that score minus the average of the three best free agents at the position, from the league rosters.
  • Start / boom lines. QB 17 / 25, RB 10 / 20, WR 10 / 20, TE 7 / 15, K 8 / 13, DL 5 / 10, LB 7 / 12, DB 6 / 11 points.
